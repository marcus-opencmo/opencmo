Most solo founders do not have a product problem. They have a distribution problem: the product works, and not enough of the right people know it exists. Ads cost money you would rather spend on runway. Cold outreach does not scale on a nights-and-weekends schedule. Content does — if you can make it without it becoming a second job.

Short vertical video is the most leveraged form of content for a founder right now, because a single long recording can feed every channel for a week.

Why is short-form video good distribution for founders?

Three reasons, none of which need a big audience to start:

  • Discovery does not depend on followers. Short-form feeds show posts to people who do not follow you yet. A good clip can reach buyers on day one.
  • One recording, many posts. A 30-minute demo or interview holds several self-contained moments. Each one becomes its own post.
  • Trust compounds. Buyers who have watched you explain the problem three times already trust you before they visit your site.

What should a founder record?

You probably already produce the raw material. Look at what you record or say out loud every week:

SourceWhat it turns into
Product demosOne clip per feature: the problem, then the fix on screen
Podcast or livestream appearancesYour strongest answers, as standalone clips
Customer calls (with permission)The questions buyers ask, answered in your own words
Talks and webinarsOpinions and frameworks, one idea per clip
Build-in-public updatesLessons and mistakes, told as short stories

What does a sustainable weekly routine look like?

The routine has to survive a busy week, so keep it small:

  1. Record once. One long piece per week: a demo, a talk, a podcast, a voice note about what you learned.
  2. Clip once. Pull out the moments that stand on their own. Our guide to turning a podcast episode into Shorts covers how to choose them.
  3. Post daily. Schedule one clip per day across your channels instead of dumping them all at once.
  4. Read the replies. Comments tell you what to record next week. The questions people ask are your next clips.

What makes founder clips actually work?

  • Lead with the problem, not the product. "Here is why your onboarding loses people" beats "We just shipped onboarding v2".
  • Be specific. Real examples, real numbers from your own work, real mistakes.
  • Caption everything. Many people watch with sound off. See our captions guide.
  • End with one next step. A link in your profile, a reply, a follow. One ask, not three.

How do you know it is working?

Do not judge a single clip. Judge the habit over a month: are more of the right people finding your profile, visiting your site, replying, signing up? Views are a weak signal on their own; conversations with potential customers are a strong one.

Distribution is a habit, not a launch. Record once a week, clip it, post it daily, and let the replies tell you what to make next.
