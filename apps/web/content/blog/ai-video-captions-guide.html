On short-form platforms, captions are not optional. A large share of people scroll with the sound off, and the first second decides whether they stay. If they cannot follow what you are saying at a glance, they are gone. Good captions keep them watching; bad captions — tiny, cluttered, covered by buttons — are almost as bad as none.

Should you burn captions in or use a subtitle file?

Burn them in. A burned-in caption is part of the video image, so it looks identical on TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and X, and it survives downloads and re-uploads. Subtitle files depend on each player, are often off by default, and cannot be styled to match your brand.

How big should captions be?

Bigger than you think. People watch on a phone at arm's length, often while moving. A few rules that hold up:

  • Large, bold type with a simple sans-serif or a strong display face.
  • High contrast: light text with a dark outline or shadow, or the reverse, so it reads over any background.
  • No thin weights. They disappear over busy footage.

Where should captions go on a vertical video?

Every app places its own interface over your video: the account name and description along the bottom, and buttons down the right side. Captions placed at the very bottom get covered. Put them in the middle-to-lower third, below the speaker's face and clear of the edges.

Framing matters here too. If the speaker drifts to the edge of a vertical crop, captions end up over their face. A crop that follows the speaker, like the one OpenCMO uses when it turns long videos into vertical clips, keeps the face and the captions in their own zones.

How many words should be on screen?

A short phrase at a time. Long sentences wrapped over two lines turn a video into reading homework. Break captions at natural pauses so each chunk is a unit of meaning, not an arbitrary cut mid-phrase.

Does highlighting the spoken word help?

Yes. Highlighting the word being spoken — in your brand color, for example — gives the eye an anchor and makes the clip feel faster. Use one highlight style consistently; changing colors on every line becomes noise.

How accurate are AI-generated captions?

Modern speech recognition handles everyday speech well, but it still trips on names, product terms, jargon and fast cross-talk. Read every caption once before you post. In OpenCMO you can fix a word directly in the transcript, and the captions update with it.
