A podcast episode is an hour of your best thinking that almost nobody will watch from start to finish. Short vertical clips are how the rest of your audience finds it. The work is not the cutting itself; it is choosing the right moments and making each one stand on its own.

This guide walks through the process we use, step by step, whether you do it by hand in an editor or let OpenCMO do the first pass for you.

What makes a podcast moment worth clipping?

A good clip makes sense to someone who has never heard of you or the show. That one test removes most candidates. Look for moments with:

  • A complete point. A claim and its reason, a question and its answer, a mistake and its lesson.
  • A strong first sentence. If the opening line needs the previous five minutes to land, the clip will not hold anyone.
  • Specifics. Names, numbers you actually said, concrete examples. Vague advice is easy to scroll past.
  • Some tension. Disagreement, a surprising answer, a confession. Polite agreement rarely travels.

Avoid inside jokes, references to earlier in the episode ("like we said before…"), and long setups. If you have to explain the context in a caption, choose a different moment.

How do you turn an episode into clips, step by step?

  1. Start from the transcript, not the timeline. Reading is faster than scrubbing. Skim for the sentences that would work as a headline and mark the passage around each one.
  2. Cut to the point. Start each clip on the strongest line, even if that is mid-answer. Trim the "so, um, yeah" at both ends.
  3. Reframe to vertical 9:16. A landscape podcast frame cropped to the center often cuts the speaker in half. The crop has to follow whoever is talking.
  4. Add captions. Burn them into the video, keep them large and high-contrast, and keep them away from the bottom edge where each app puts its buttons.
  5. Watch every clip once before posting. Check the first second, the framing and the last word. Most bad clips are bad in one of those three places.
  6. Post over time. Spread the clips across days so each one gets its own chance, and link back to the full episode.

How do you keep the speaker in frame when going vertical?

Most podcasts are filmed wide, with two or more people sitting side by side. A fixed center crop works only when the person speaking happens to sit in the middle. In practice they rarely do, and a fixed crop leaves half a face at the edge of the screen.

The fix is a crop that tracks faces and moves to whoever is talking. If you edit by hand, that means keyframing the crop position at every change of speaker. OpenCMO does this automatically with face tracking, which is the single biggest reason clips look finished rather than cropped.

What should podcast captions look like?

Captions on short clips are part of the picture, not an accessibility afterthought. Keep them short — a few words at a time — and readable at a glance on a phone. We go deeper on size, placement and timing in our guide to captions people actually read.

How often should you post the clips?

One episode can feed your short-form channels for a week or more. Posting clips over several days gives each one its own window and keeps your profile active between episodes. If you are a founder using clips for distribution, our piece on short-form video for founders covers how this fits into a weekly routine.

The short version: choose moments that stand alone, cut straight to the point, keep the speaker in frame and caption everything. Do that consistently and every long episode becomes a week of short posts.
